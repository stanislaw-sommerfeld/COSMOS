Orbital Motion Planning

COSMOS

Energy-Aware Formation Reconfiguration via Covariant Optimization in the Clohessy–Wiltshire Metric

Make CHOMP's covariant gradient aware of orbital mechanics — so a generic path smoother becomes a fuel-optimal orbital maneuver planner. Now generalized to time-varying dynamics around eccentric orbits.

The key idea

Smoothing the wrong thing

Standard trajectory smoothing minimizes acceleration. But in orbit the dynamics already supply acceleration for free — so penalizing raw acceleration optimizes a quantity that has little to do with fuel.

Standard CHOMP
\(A^{\top}A\)
Energy of the raw acceleration — dynamics-blind.
→
COSMOS
\(B^{\top}B\)
Energy of the actual thrust, \(B = D_2 - G_{\mathrm{CW}}\). Fuel-aware.
$$U(\xi) = \lambda\,F_{\text{fuel}} + F_{\text{obs}} + \mu\,F_{\text{agents}} \qquad \xi \leftarrow \xi - \alpha\,(B^{\top}B)^{-1}\nabla U \qquad F_{\text{fuel}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\int \big\lVert \ddot{\xi} - f_{\text{CW}} \big\rVert^{2}\, dt$$
We optimize control energy (L2) — the smooth quantity that makes the Riemannian metric and the closed-form optimum possible — and report the realized Δv (L1), the true propellant currency. As the orbital rate \(n \to 0\), \(B \to D_2\) and COSMOS reduces to ordinary free-space CHOMP.

01 · generality, where it pays

A time-varying metric for eccentric orbits

\(B = D_2 - G(t)\)  ·  Tschauner–Hempel

Clohessy–Wiltshire assumes a circular reference orbit. Around an eccentric orbit the relative dynamics become time-varying, and a CW-“passive” relative orbit drifts by many times its own amplitude per revolution. Feeding the planner the correct time-varying block \(G(t)\) — the same construction, nothing else changed — and scoring against the full nonlinear relative dynamics, the Δv saving climbs from ~0% at \(e=0\) (so CW is validated for circular LEO) to ~16% at \(e=0.3\). The metric matters exactly when the dynamics do.

# clone & install (editable)
git clone https://github.com/stanislaw-sommerfeld/COSMOS.git
cd COSMOS
python -m venv venv && source venv/bin/activate
pip install -e .            # add ".[viz]" for the pygame viewer
# run the whole framework at once (figures + results + tests)
python run_all.py

# ...or the unified driver — configured by flags
cosmos --units si                              # the mission, real units
cosmos --metric free --safety none --assign fixed   # dynamics-blind baseline
cosmos --scenario debris --dim 3 --units si    # 3D debris rendezvous

# reproduce the headline figures

python -m cosmos.cosmos_optimal  # 1.00× LQ optimum + the 15× pitfall
python -m cosmos.cosmos_safe     # 15× → 1.69× safety fix

Contribution 01 · the core idea

Fuel-aware Clohessy–Wiltshire metric

Standard CHOMP measures “smoothness” as the energy of the raw acceleration, \(\tfrac12\int\lVert\ddot\xi\rVert^2\). In orbit that is the wrong currency: the dynamics already supply acceleration for free, so the thruster only has to provide the difference between the acceleration you want and what gravity gives you.

$$F_{\text{fuel}}=\tfrac12\!\int\big\lVert\ddot\xi-f_{\text{CW}}(\xi,\dot\xi)\big\rVert^2dt,\qquad u=B\xi+c_0,\quad B=D_2-G_{\text{CW}}$$

Because the Clohessy–Wiltshire dynamics are linear, the control \(u\) is linear in the trajectory, and the smoothness metric becomes \(M=B^{\top}B\). The covariant descent \(\xi\leftarrow\xi-\alpha\,M^{-1}\nabla U\) then minimizes true thrust energy.

Why it is new

  • CHOMP (Ratliff 2009) uses the dynamics-blind metric \(A^{\top}A\); classical astrodynamics has the LQ/CW optimum but no obstacle-aware planner. COSMOS is the bridge: the relative dynamics go into the covariant preconditioner itself.
  • The planner spontaneously discovers natural coast arcs — it rides the orbital drift instead of fighting it.

Result & limits

  • ~40% ± 10% Δv saved vs dynamics-blind planning, over 10 random scenarios.
  • Reduces exactly to free-space CHOMP as the orbital rate \(n\to0\) (a locked regression test).
  • Limited to linear relative dynamics and L2 control energy (we report the realized L1 Δv) — honest for the low-thrust regime.

Contribution 02 · Module A

Provable optimality against the LQ / Gramian optimum

The CW dynamics are linear and the fuel cost is quadratic, so the minimum-energy transfer is a Linear-Quadratic problem with a closed-form optimum via the controllability Gramian \(W(T)\):

$$J^\star=(s_f-\Phi(T)s_0)^{\top}W(T)^{-1}(s_f-\Phi(T)s_0)$$

This is an analytical ground truth: no planner can beat it. Fuel-aware CHOMP recovers it to 1.00×; a dynamics-blind planner sits at 1.79×; and a naive Euclidean keep-out projection balloons to 15× — the pitfall Module B then fixes.

Why it matters

  • It turns “our planner seems good” into “our planner is provably at the optimum” in the unconstrained case.
  • It exposes, quantitatively, that the metric — not the solver — is what buys the fuel saving.

Contribution 03 · Module B

Metric-aware safety (Riemannian projection)

Enforcing a keep-out by snapping a waypoint radially out (Euclidean) ignores the fuel metric and costs 15× the optimal energy. Module B instead projects onto the constraint in the metric \(M=B^{\top}B\) — a closed-form minimum-fuel correction:

$$\delta\xi=\frac{M^{-1}a\,(b-a^{\top}\xi)}{a^{\top}M^{-1}a}$$

for a linearized keep-out \(a^{\top}\xi\ge b\). This cuts the safety premium from 15× to 1.69× while staying strictly collision-free.

Why it is new & where it goes

  • Projection methods are standard; doing it in the dynamics-induced Riemannian metric, in closed form, is the novelty.
  • Extended in E3 to moving inter-agent constraints and in E5 to chance constraints.
  • Limits: static, spherical keep-outs, linearized per waypoint.

Extension E1 · generality where it pays

Time-varying metric for eccentric orbits (Tschauner–Hempel)

CW assumes a circular reference orbit. Around an eccentric orbit the linearized relative dynamics are time-varying — the coupling block depends on the true anomaly — so the construction becomes \(B=D_2-G(t)\). Everything else (preconditioner, closed-form optimum, projections) is unchanged.

$$\ddot x=K_x(t)\,x+K_v(t)\,\dot x+u,\qquad B=D_2-G(t)$$

The honest, quantitative payoff

  • A CW-“passive” relative orbit drifts by many times its amplitude per revolution when the reference is eccentric — CW is genuinely the wrong metric there.
  • Scored against the full nonlinear relative dynamics, the Δv saving of the correct time-varying metric vs CW grows from ~0% at \(e=0\) (validating CW for circular LEO) to ~16% at \(e=0.3\).
  • Holds in 3D too: the out-of-plane (cross-track) mode becomes the true time-varying gravity gradient \(z''=-(\mu/r^3)z\), and the saving curve is essentially the same with the cross-track component included.
  • Reduces to CW bit-for-bit at \(e=0\), in both 2D and 3D (regression tests).

This is why the project is not a toy: the “metric = dynamics” claim is demonstrated where it matters, not merely asserted.

Extension E2 · a second-order guarantee

The preconditioner is the exact Gauss–Newton step

The unconstrained fuel cost \(U=\tfrac12\lVert B\xi+c_0\rVert^2\) is exactly quadratic, so its Hessian is \(H=B^{\top}B=M\) — precisely the covariant preconditioner. One unit step is therefore a full Newton step and lands on the optimum from any start:

$$\xi^\star=\xi_0-M^{-1}\nabla U(\xi_0)\quad\text{(exact, one iteration)}$$

Why it matters

  • It reframes COSMOS from “a smoother that converges” to a second-order solver for the LQ transfer — the 1.00× result is a property of the method, not of step-size or iteration budget.
  • It is also what makes the per-step replanning in E4 (MPC) essentially free.

Extension E3 · swarm safety

Metric-aware inter-agent safety (coupled Riemannian projection)

A swarm's real hazard is the other agents. The separation constraint \(\lVert x_i(t)-x_j(t)\rVert\ge r\) couples two trajectories at the same instant. Stacking \(z=[\xi_i;\xi_j]\) with the block-diagonal metric \(\mathrm{diag}(M,M)\), the minimum-fuel correction is again closed form and splits into two per-agent pushes sharing one multiplier:

$$\lambda=\frac{b-a^{\top}z}{a_i^{\top}M^{-1}a_i+a_j^{\top}M^{-1}a_j},\quad \xi_i\mathrel{+}=\lambda M^{-1}a_i,\ \xi_j\mathrel{+}=\lambda M^{-1}a_j$$

Each agent moves the way that is cheapest in its own fuel metric. Result: strictly collision-free at ~1.3× lower Δv than the metric-blind “push both apart equally” — the swarm analogue of Module B.

Extension E4 · closed loop

Receding-horizon replanning (MPC-ready)

Every other result is open-loop. Under process noise an open-loop plan drifts off. Wrapping the fuel-aware solver in a receding-horizon loop — execute one step, re-plan from the measured state, repeat — cuts the terminal error ~10× for a modest Δv premium.

The connection to E2

  • Each re-plan is essentially one Gauss–Newton step (E2), so MPC is cheap to run: the metric is MPC-ready out of the box.
  • Honest scope: this is standard receding-horizon control; the novelty is that COSMOS's own covariant solver is the inner planner.

Extension E5 · robustness

Chance-constrained keep-outs under navigation uncertainty

The keep-out radius is treated as exact, but position is known only up to navigation error \(\sigma\). Requiring the true position to stay outside with probability \(\ge\beta\) becomes a deterministic constraint on the mean, with an inflated radius:

$$r_{\text{safe}}=r_{\text{phys}}+\Phi^{-1}(\beta)\,\sigma$$

Planning against \(r_{\text{safe}}\) with the same Module B projection then guarantees the chance constraint — zero new machinery. Monte Carlo confirms the realized safety tracks the target \(\beta\), and Δv rises smoothly with the safety level: a tunable safety/fuel trade.

Validity · honesty

When does the eccentric metric actually pay?

A single headline number (“16% at \(e=0.3\)”) invites the fair question: robust, or cherry-picked? This study answers it with a regime map rather than a point. It sweeps a family of rest-to-rest transfers over two axes — reference-orbit eccentricity and transfer amplitude — keeping the transfer shape fixed, and scores the COSMOS plan (correct time-varying metric) against the dynamics-blind plan (circular / CW metric) under the same full nonlinear ground truth.

What it shows

  • The saving is positive and grows with eccentricity across the whole small-to-moderate amplitude envelope (3–16%) — so the headline result is a regime, not a lucky point.
  • The advantage tracks the dynamics mismatch: the more the circular assumption is wrong, the more the correct metric helps.
  • Documented limit (honest): Tschauner–Hempel is a linearization. For very large or strongly-radial transfers the linear model itself departs from the true nonlinear motion, so no linear metric (CW or TH) is the right object there — a limit of linear relative-motion planning, surfaced rather than hidden.

Showing the operating envelope — including where the method does not apply — is exactly what makes a contribution credible rather than oversold.

Mission · credible numbers

A realistic SI mission on an eccentric orbit

Most modules report normalized quantities. This one puts a believable number on the table: a four-chaser debris rendezvous around a real eccentric reference orbit — perigee 600 km, apogee 8000 km, so semi-major axis ≈ 10 670 km, eccentricity \(e=0.35\), period ≈ 183 min — with the formation at the hundred-metre scale.

Why this is in the valid regime

  • The formation amplitude is ~\(10^{-5}\) of the orbit radius, so the Tschauner–Hempel linearization is firmly valid (exactly the envelope the validity map endorses).
  • Real missions do fly eccentric reference orbits (transfer/parking orbits, Molniya-class, eccentric science orbits).

Result

  • COSMOS (correct time-varying metric): ~1.1 m/s per chaser.
  • Dynamics-blind (circular metric): ~1.4 m/s per chaser.
  • +19.5 % propellant saved, scored under the full nonlinear relative motion — a credible low-thrust reconfiguration budget.

Robustness · trust

Numerical robustness across the discretization

A method is only trustworthy if it behaves predictably as the knobs change. Sweeping the discretization \(N\) from 20 to 200:

  • The metric's condition number grows \(\sim N^2\) — the known behaviour of a finite-difference Laplacian — so a naive matrix inverse would lose digits.
  • COSMOS forms the preconditioner through a Cholesky factorization, which keeps the covariant descent exact: it lands on the closed-form optimum to \(\sim 10^{-10}\) at every \(N\).
  • The energy ratio to the analytical LQ optimum stays within a fraction of a percent of 1.0; the mild drift at large \(N\) is the expected discretization effect, not solver error.
  • The fast Van Loan Gramian agrees with the legacy quadrature to its own discretization error and is symmetric by construction.

Nothing here changes a result — it documents the operating characteristics so the headline numbers can be trusted away from the single tested configuration.

Benchmark · the justification

COSMOS vs SCP/SQP vs the LQ optimum

To show the problem is non-trivial and the method performant, COSMOS is compared on the same hard (non-convex keep-out) problem against the baselines a reviewer demands:

  • LQ (analytic) — the closed-form unconstrained optimum. It violates the keep-out (it has no obstacle notion), so a real constrained planner is required.
  • SCP / SQP — a general-purpose sequential-quadratic-programming solver (scipy SLSQP) minimizing the exact control energy subject to the hard keep-out. The serious alternative.
  • COSMOS — the fuel metric + closed-form Riemannian projection, with adaptive convergence.

Findings

  • Where SLSQP converges (small horizons), COSMOS matches its optimum to <1% in Δv and stays strictly collision-free — near-optimal, not a heuristic.
  • COSMOS scales far better: SLSQP factorizes dense Jacobians/Hessians that grow with the horizon (roughly cubic per iteration); COSMOS's covariant step is a fixed structured solve. At \(N\!\approx\!150\) COSMOS is 15–18× faster and still feasible, while SLSQP exhausts its budget and returns an infeasible trajectory that cuts the keep-out.

That is the real justification: a planner that reaches a general optimizer's quality on the hard, coupled, safety-constrained problem at a fraction of the cost — because the metric encodes the dynamics.
